# Project 15: An Algerian-Dialect Conversational System for Smoking Reduction and Youth Support

## Introduction and Objective
Smoking remains a pressing public health issue in Algeria, particularly among young people, with prevalence rates disproportionately higher among males. Despite national campaigns and cessation programs, many young smokers still struggle due to cultural normalization, peer pressure, lack of tailored interventions, and limited access to effective support tools.

This project aims to develop an LLM-powered assistant that interacts in Algerian dialect (Darija), providing culturally relevant and understandable support. The system helps young smokers:

- Understand the health risks of smoking
- Explore practical cessation strategies
- Stay motivated through conversational, personalized guidance

By leveraging conversational AI, the project seeks to deliver an accessible, trustworthy, and user-friendly assistant that complements overburdened health services.

## Project Description
The project combines:

- Data collection and curation
- LLM evaluation and fine-tuning
- Optimization for mobile devices or lightweight web deployment

Its core is a multifunctional support environment tailored to young smokers, offering evidence-based educational content, personalized prompts, and simulated counselling dialogues adapted to Algeria's linguistic and cultural context.

## Core Components

### A. Data Collection and Curation
- Collect and curate:
  - Health resources in Arabic and French about smoking effects and cessation
  - Youth-focused campaign materials and awareness content from Algerian health authorities
  - User-generated Q&A samples and anonymized counselling transcripts (where ethically available)
- Translate, adapt, and align resources into Algerian dialect for natural conversational use.

### B. LLM-Based Model Training and Fine-Tuning
- Evaluate open/free LLMs for Arabic dialect comprehension and generation.
- Fine-tune the most promising model using Algerian dialect corpora and smoking-related data.
- Optimize for lightweight deployment on constrained infrastructure (smartphones, rural internet).

### C. Application Features
- **Interactive Counselling Assistant:**
  - Motivational support
  - Answers to common questions (cravings, withdrawal symptoms, alternative habits)
  - Practical behavior-change strategies
- **Personalized Goal Setting:**
  - Set gradual reduction goals
  - Track progress over time
- **Educational Modules:**
  - Short, engaging content (text and optionally audio)
  - Health risks, financial impacts, and social consequences of smoking
- **Gamified Rewards System (optional):**
  - Badges, streaks, and micro-challenges
- **Emergency Support Links:**
  - Quick access to hotlines or local clinics for high-risk situations

### D. Safety, Security, and Trustworthiness
- Process sensitive user inputs securely.
- Implement fact-checking pipelines to ensure medical accuracy aligned with WHO and Algerian Ministry of Health guidance.
- Add safeguards against harmful or biased outputs.
- Provide clear disclaimers that the assistant does not replace professional medical advice.

## System Evaluation
Evaluation will focus on:

- **Linguistic Quality:** coherence, clarity, and naturalness of Algerian dialect responses
- **Behavioural Impact:** smoking reduction effectiveness (self-reported surveys, pilot studies)
- **Usability:** satisfaction, accessibility, and simplicity on limited devices
- **Trustworthiness:** accuracy and reliability of health outputs (minimized hallucinations, safety compliance)

## Expected Outcomes
- A mobile/web assistant that supports smoking reduction in Algerian dialect
- A curated dataset of Algerian dialect smoking-related health conversations
- Technical documentation and a research paper on LLM-based public health interventions in North Africa

## Expected Impact
This project is expected to improve public health outcomes by directly addressing youth smoking in Algeria. By offering culturally relevant, dialect-based, and accessible support, it can empower healthier choices, reduce smoking prevalence, and lower long-term healthcare costs.

It can also serve as a blueprint for future dialect-based health intervention systems across Algeria and the wider Arab world.
